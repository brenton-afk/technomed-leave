VA LCP™ Clavicle Plate 2.7

Surgical Technique & Plate Selection Guide

Three plate types. One 2.7 mm screw platform.

Anatomically contoured lateral, shaft and medial plates — each left and right. VA Combi holes take VA locking screws for angular stability and cortex screws for compression. All screw holes accept 2.7 mm screws.

Tap a step to open it. This is a condensed field reference — 13 technique steps plus optional implant removal. Instruction by a surgeon experienced with these products is required.

1PreparationPositioning & imaging
Supine, radiolucent table
Beach chair, 30°–45° tilt

Positioning is surgeon preference. Use a supine position on a radiolucent table, or a beach chair position with 30°–45° of tilt.

Place a small roll or folded towel between the scapulae to retract the shoulders and assist reduction. Turn the head away from the operative side and support it on a head rest.

Prep the entire upper extremity, upper chest wall and hemithorax — including the sternum and sternoclavicular articulation.

AP and axial fluoroscopic visualisation is recommended. For medial fractures, position the C-arm perpendicular to the SC joint.

■

Check C-arm access and take trial images before draping to confirm the views can be obtained.

2ApproachIncision & nerve protection
Longitudinal incision
Vertical incision

Determine incision length and location based on the fracture pattern, fracture location and planned fixation.

The medial, intermediate and lateral supraclavicular nerves run deep to the platysma then cross the clavicle. Perform subcutaneous dissection carefully, identify the major sensory branches and protect them with small vessel loops throughout the case.

Divide the platysma carefully — the supraclavicular nerves may still lie deep to it depending on the cephalad level of dissection. Expose clavicular periosteum at the deltotrapezial fascia and pectoralis origin.

Dissection should be epiperiosteal. Keep periosteal dissection minimal — just enough to expose the fracture. For medial fractures, elevate the sternocleidomastoid.

▲

The periosteum of bone fragments must not be completely detached — preserve the bony blood supply. Do not strip comminuted fragments.

3Reduce & Temporarily FixRestore length, axis, rotation
Pointed reduction forceps, nerves protected with vessel loops

After exposure, distract the two main fragments and restore clavicular length. For angled or oblique bone ends, reduce with pointed or serrated reduction forceps. Restore normal length, axis angulation and rotation.

Reduce large comminuted fragments and hold them with small pointed bone clamps. Plan temporary fixation so it does not interfere with definitive fixation.

Other options for holding reduction
  • Continuous compression implants
  • Independent lag screws
  • Lag screws through the plate
  • Temporary K-wire fixation — protect all critical structures
■

Bridging option. The plates can be used for biological, bridging osteosynthesis — only the main fragments are reduced and the fracture zone is not engaged with any screws.

4Determine Plate Type & ShapeTemplating
Templates: lateral · shaft · medial · shaft XL

Types: lateral, shaft, medial. Shapes: lateral and shaft come in CS1, CS2 and CS3; the medial plate is one size. See the Plates tab for the full size chart.

Lateral and shaft templates and plates sit on the superior aspect of the clavicle. The medial end of the medial template and plate sits on the anterior aspect.

With the segments in anatomic alignment, insert the template and assess fit and suitability for fixation of the main fragments. Screw hole positions of the matching plate are marked on the template.

Templates can be temporarily fixed with clamps, or in the lateral clavicle by passing a K-wire up to 2.0 mm or a compression wire through the template hole. Confirm reduction, fit, planned screw positions and shoulder function — use fluoroscopy if needed.

If the plan calls for axial dynamic compression, position the template so there is at least one VA Combi hole in each main fragment.

5Select Plate Type & ShapeConfirm fit
Confirm plate fit on the reduced clavicle

Select the plate type and shape matching the template used. If templates were not used, select on clavicle morphology and fracture location.

With the segments in anatomic alignment, confirm the plate fits the clavicle and is appropriate for fixation of the main fragments.

■

Plates are available in stainless steel and titanium alloy.

6Adapt Plate to BoneOptional · max 10°
In-plane bending
Torsional bending

Check plate fit. Because clavicle shape and length vary widely, slight bending may be needed.

In-plane bending — use the bending pliers. Only possible on the lateral and medial plates, and only at the bending notch. Insert the plate into the slots in the front of the plier jaws, centred over the notch. For extra leverage, loosen the adjustment screw so the handles sit closer together, then make a series of small bends, threading the screw about half a turn each time.

Out-of-plane bending — use bending pliers or bending irons. With irons, hold the plate in the middle slot of the closed iron and position the middle slot of the open iron at the segment to be bent.

Tab bending — the bending pliers can also adjust the tab on the medial plates.

Torsional bending — use bending irons. Hold the plate in the closed iron, position the open iron along the plate and rotate the two irons. Plates may be twisted up to 10°.

▲

Do not bend more than 10° in-plane or out-of-plane — excessive bending weakens the plate and can cause premature failure.

Avoid reverse bending (bending then straightening). Do not make an acute bend directly over a screw hole — check the VA portion of adjacent holes with a variable angle drill guide after bending to confirm they are not deformed.

Nominal screw angle is set by plate design and screw length. If the plate is contoured and/or a screw longer than 40 mm is used, take care that screws do not collide — use image intensification.

■

Correct handling. If the implant shape must be altered, the device should not be bent sharply, bent backwards, notched or scratched. Such manipulations produce surface defects and concentrate stress in the core of the implant, which may eventually cause the product to fail.

7Plate Insertion & Temporary FixationFour holding options
A cortex/metaphyseal screw · B forceps · C compression wire · D K-wire

Position the plate on the reduced bone and hold it temporarily with any of:

  • A — Cortex screw or metaphyseal screw
  • B — Reduction forceps / serrated clamps
  • C — Compression wire
  • D — K-wire

Centre the compression wire within the plate hole to minimise shifting of plate position as the wire pulls the plate to bone.

A K-wire up to 2.0 mm can go through the lateral suture hole or K-wire hole as a reference to visualise the lateral clavicle and aid plate placement.

After insertion, confirm fit and bone alignment with fluoroscopy.

8Screw ConfigurationWhich screw, which hole
Fig. 4 — ① VA Combi holes ② VA locking holes
Fig. 5 — VA locking screw ±15°

All screw holes in the VA LCP Clavicle Plates accept 2.7 mm screws. Determine the combination required for fixation, and when planning location and length consider screw collision and over-penetration.

Cortex screws — use in the non-threaded DCU portion of the VA Combi hole ① in the neutral/centred position, or eccentrically for compression. In a VA locking hole ② they may only be used at the nominal position; they cannot be placed eccentrically and the head will not sit flush.

VA locking screws — use in VA locking holes ② at nominal or variable angle (±15°), and in the threaded portion of VA Combi holes. Not in the non-threaded DCU portion.

Metaphyseal screws — provide compression with a low-profile head that sits flush. Same shaft thread as VA locking screws; use in VA locking holes ② and the threaded portion of VA Combi holes ① at the nominal angle only. Not in the DCU portion.

■

If mixing VA locking, cortex and metaphyseal screws, insert the cortex or metaphyseal screws first, next to the fracture. This pulls the plate to the bone so it sits flush and enhances construct stability — especially where high implant load is expected.

2.7 mm locking screws with Head LCP 3.5 are not compatible with the VA locking holes of this system.

▲

WARNING — avoid penetration of vital neurovascular structures posterior to the clavicle.

9Screw Insertion — Cortex2.7 mm cortex screws
Fig. 6 — neutral / centred
Fig. 7 — eccentric, for compression
Hole preparation

Neutral / centred: thread the neutral sleeve adapter 2.7 mm onto the 2.0 mm end of the non-locking drill guide 2.7 mm and place the guide tip in the centre of the DCU hole. No compression occurs across the fracture.

Dynamic compression: do not use the neutral sleeve adapter. Place the 2.0 mm end of the drill guide eccentrically at the edge of the DCU portion, away from the fracture. Compression occurs as the cortex screw is tightened and the head slides across the compression hole.

Fig. 8 — read depth directly from the calibrated drill shaft

Drill to depth with the Ø 2.0 mm drill bit. The 2.0 mm drill bits are calibrated so depth can be read directly off the drill shaft.

Depth measurement

After drilling, remove the guide, insert the depth gauge tip through the hole and measure. For bicortical measurement, pass the tip through both cortices and hook the far cortex.

  • 03.111.005 — slide the black portion toward the bone until it stops; read the length from the line on the silver slider. No subtraction needed.
  • 03.133.080 — pull the knob up until it stops; read length from the top edge of the metal sleeve.
Screw insertion

Attach the T8 StarDrive™ screwdriver shaft (314.467) to the universal screwdriver handle. Pick the screw from the caddy with the driver tip and advance it until fully seated in the plate. Cortex screws may also be inserted using power.

10Screw Insertion — VA Locking & MetaphysealNominal & variable angle
Fig. 9 — threaded drill guide 03.133.008
Fig. 10 — coaxial VA LCP drill sleeve 03.211.004
Nominal angle drilling

Screw the threaded drill guide 2.0 mm into the screw hole perpendicular to the plate until fully seated. To ease threading, engage the guide with a quarter turn counterclockwise until the starting threads meet, then turn clockwise. The T8 StarDrive shaft can be inserted into the back of the threaded guide to help drive it in.

Nominal angle is determined by plate design. Cortex screw heads will not be flush in a locking hole — to reduce head protrusion, use a low-profile metaphyseal screw at nominal angle. Drill to depth with the Ø 2.0 mm drill bit; 03.133.100 is calibrated for direct reading with guide 03.133.008.

VA drill guide 03.133.007 — cone end and spherical tip
Variable angle drilling — VA locking screws only

Insert the VA drill guide into the VA locking hole. It has a VA cone on one side and a VA spherical tip on the other.

Cone end: press firmly so the tip keys securely into the cloverleaf portion of the hole. The notches on top of the cone mark tip orientation. The cone provides ±15° of angulation.

Spherical tip: for freehand drilling, press gently into the VA hole — the lip engages the hole to give tactile feedback of angulation. Maintain light pressure at the desired angle. To guarantee 15°, use the cone end.

Depth measurement
■

Using depth gauge 03.133.080 for 2.7 mm VA locking screws, subtract 2 mm from the indicated length to get the correct screw length. Depth gauge 03.111.005 requires no subtraction.

Screw insertion

Attach the 1.2 Nm torque limiter to the universal screwdriver handle, pick the screw from the caddy and advance it into the hole. The TLA gives an audible click once the torque value is reached, indicating the screw is seated and locked.

To insert under power, use the T8 StarDrive shaft attached to the 1.2 Nm TLA. Confirm screw position and length before final tightening. Final tightening must be done manually or at low speed using the 1.2 Nm TLA.

Always use a 1.2 Nm torque limiting attachment when inserting VA locking screws.

Do not lock screws using power tools without the 1.2 Nm TLA or at high speed — this may damage the screwdriver and strip the screw head, making implant removal difficult.

Verify the drill bit angle under image intensification. Drilling consecutive holes off-axis can cause screws to collide.

■

Metaphyseal screws can only be inserted in VA locking holes at the nominal angle. VA locking screws will not be flush with the plate unless placed at nominal angle.

11Soft Tissue AttachmentOptional · lateral plate
Suture holes on the lateral and anterior aspects

Lateral plates have suture holes on the lateral and anterior aspects to reattach ruptured ligaments or muscles.

  • Anterior holes — attach the anterior deltoid
  • Lateral holes — attach the superior acromioclavicular ligament or other soft tissue

Use multiple suture holes for added stability. Taper point suture needles sized 26 mm ½ C radius are recommended.

■

The suture holes are undercut to allow needle passage, but the undercut may be blocked depending on patient anatomy — in that case insert the suture before final plate fixation.

12Reduction & Fixation ConfirmationDirect vision + fluoroscopy
Confirm reduction and screw position

Assess final reduction and fixation by both direct visualisation and image intensification. Inspect the construct by rechecking every screw before closing to verify they are secure.

Use AP and additional fluoroscopic views to confirm reduction and appropriate positioning of plate and screws. Confirm full shoulder range of motion and stability of the fixation.

■

A VA locking screw will not be flush with the plate unless placed at nominal angle. Cortex screw heads will not be flush when inserted into VA locking holes.

13Surgical ClosureLayered closure

Thoroughly irrigate the wound before closure. Perform a careful layered closure — the trapezial-deltoid fascia can often be approximated over the plate. Close the platysma and the subcutaneous tissue as separate layers.

✕Implant RemovalOptional
Unlock all screws before removing any

Unlock all screws from the plate first, then remove the screws completely from the bone. This prevents the plate rotating with the last locking screw as it is unlocked.

If a screw cannot be removed with the screwdriver, insert the conical extraction screw (left-handed thread) into the screw head using the handle with quick coupling and loosen counterclockwise.
