The role in a nutshell
You are the keeper of the system
The team makes the clinical calls. Your job is to keep everything running smoothly around them — consistently, every day.
It's about upkeep, not decisions
The team handles ~95% on its own. You keep the background tight so nothing slips.
Stay on top of the trickle
Bookings, WhatsApp and list orders flow in constantly. Kept current daily it's easy — let it drift and it's very hard to recover.
Be the one with the full picture
A calm coordinator who keeps the whole team pointed the right way.
Consistency beats instinct
You don't need Brent's gut feel — just run the routines every day. That's a system, not a personality trait.
How you work
Stay free — you're the floating resource
A coordination hub, not another pair of case hands. Where possible,
you don't attend cases yourself
— so you're free to:
Relieve staff during long cases.
Run resupply
Move stock and trays between hospitals.
Field the messages
Answer queries and keep WhatsApp moving.
Sort the logistics
Keep things flowing quietly in the background.
Don't get lumbered:
if you end up covering a case, hand your team-leader duties to someone else for that time. The role must never collapse back onto one person.

Definition of done
New booking checklist
A booking is
not finished when it's in the calendar
. It's finished when every step below is done.
Tap a row to mark it complete.
1 · Calendar entry — first, always
Calendar is king. Enter in the standard format the moment the booking is known.
2 · Acknowledge on WhatsApp
Reply on the WhatsApp group so the nursing/hospital team knows we've got it. (Bookings emails from Sharon & CNS don't need a reply.)
3 · Case specifics — what does it need?
Is it an extension of an existing construct? A standard consignment kit, or are there special instrument/implant requests?
4 · Implant sufficiency
Confirm we have sufficient implants/stock for the case.
5 · Kit & loan set logistics
Book loan sets and confirm timing. Arrange any kit transfer from another hospital, and sort the comms/turnaround to get kits restocked and ready in time.
6 · Delegate case attendance
Pivot to the Clinical Logistics group and agree who covers the case (see Lists & Staff tab).
7 · Coverage confirmed & locked
The assigned rep has acknowledged. The plan is in the calendar. Done.
When Toni's not in the office:
steps 3–5 are the ones most often missed. Do not skip them — this checklist is what replaces Toni's attentive eye on a day she's not there.
Working with Toni:
on Toni's booking days she liaises with the team leader on each case, so that everything is available and ready — with the team leader providing the
clinical-knowledge support

Direct bookings by the team:
if a clinical team member takes a booking straight from a surgeon, nurse or registrar, they
book it in the calendar themselves
and report it to the team leader — the first port of call for follow-up on set movements, implant requirements, etc. If the team leader is swamped, the team member carries out the follow-up themselves and updates the relevant WhatsApp group.
Every evening · by 5pm
List orders & theatre allocations
A core daily duty. Get the next day's lists sorted and published so everyone walks in knowing exactly where they're going.
Obtain the list orders
Collect the theatre list orders for the next day from each hospital, no later than 5pm.
Allocate staff to each list
Assign a rep to each theatre/list based on skill mix, case timings & list order, and staff preferences.
Publish on Technomed Main
Post the list orders together with the staff allocation for each list, so the whole team has the plan for tomorrow.

Allocating cases & resolving clashes
Run these factors in order. The call is a judgement, but the sequence stops anything being overlooked.
Competency (hard filter)
Who is actually trained to cover this case type? Rules people out first. (Mat — no ortho. Brent — no ortho nailing. Aimee — learning spine. April — ortho, casual.)
Availability
Who is genuinely free — including personal commitments and rostered patterns.
Timing, list order & location
Line up start times, list order and hospitals so overlapping cases don't clash.
Confirm commitments & availability — the step that saves you
Before locking: does anyone already have something on that day? Is the ortho director in or out? Ask explicitly — don't assume.
Brent's key-surgeon attendance, travel/geography, load balancing, and development opportunities (putting a learner in when it's safe).
Why step 4 matters:
the classic near-miss is committing a rep to a case, then discovering a director had an undisclosed case or was returning from leave unannounced. The clash is almost always an
information gap, not a bad decision
. Confirm before you lock.

Restock & inter-hospital logistics
Keeping trays and stock flowing is a background job the team leader owns — but delegates the legwork.
Liaise with both loans teams
Stay in regular contact with the two loans teams regarding restock needs and tray movements.
Delegate distribution & dispatch
Systematically hand out the distribution and dispatch of restock to the clinical team — assign it, don't absorb it all yourself.
Run the background logistics
As the floating resource, move trays and run resupply between hospitals so nothing stalls a case.
Keep restock status visible
Keep the loans and TM Usage Case Records groups current, so tray readiness is always known at a glance.
Delegate, don't martyr:
restock is a team activity you coordinate, not a solo chore. Spreading it keeps you free to float — and stops the role becoming a bottleneck.
